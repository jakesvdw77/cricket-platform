# 101 — Match Results: Our-Side Scorecard (batting, bowling, fielding, extras, standouts, player of the match)

**Depends on:** `100-match-results-core.md` (merged, PR #116: `match_result`, `match_innings`, the draft/publish gate, `ResultDraft`, `ResultValidator`, the data-driven wizard), `001-tenancy-identity-model.md` (`Person`, `PlayerProfile`: one profile per person per club, `UNIQUE (person_id, club_id)`), `029-league-management.md` and `076`/`093` (team selection: `MatchSide`, `MatchSidePlayer` with its planned `batting_order`, role, 12th man), `097-import-framework.md` (confirm-before-write principle), `docs/standards/*.md`.
**Status:** draft — written 2026-10-14 from the user's requirements for slice 101 of `100`'s roadmap. Builds **our side's scorecard only** for **regular innings only**. Super-over scorecards are split out into [`105`](#rollout-notes) (number chosen here; `102` to `104` stay as `100` outlined them).

## Problem & Goals

`100` captures what happened (outcome, toss, innings totals) but not who did it. There is no batting, bowling or fielding record, so there is nothing to feed player statistics (`103`), no standouts or player of the match, and the batting order a coach actually used on the day is lost (`093` shows the announced plan only, and its roadmap item waits on this slice). The scorecard must also be quick to enter: a manager copies it from a paper or app scorecard, usually on a phone, often with last-minute player changes, borrowed players and a scorecard that does not quite add up.

Goals:
- **A. Our side's scorecard** per regular innings: batting (order, runs, balls, fours, sixes, how out), bowling (balls, maidens, runs, wickets, wides, no-balls), fielding (catches, stumpings, run-outs) and a full extras breakdown (wides, no-balls, byes, leg byes, penalty runs).
- **B. Pre-filled from the selection, never constrained by it:** the line-up and batting order come from `MatchSidePlayer` (the announced or proposed team); every part can be changed.
- **C. Last-minute changes and non-registered players:** a row-level player swap, and free-text names for guests and borrowed players with a clear stats rule.
- **D. Minimal clicks:** a keyboard-first grid that is also phone-first, with defaults (batting order from the line-up, did-not-bat for the rest, bowlers from the line-up) and a form driven by the wickets already known from the result.
- **E. Actual batting order recorded** (planned order kept as a snapshot) for the rotation item in `093`'s roadmap.
- **F. Reconciliation against the result,** as acknowledgeable warnings in the shared `ResultValidator` (so `104` imports get them too).
- **G. Standouts and player of the match,** in an Awards step. Standouts are **suggestions** computed by relative rules (no thresholds to configure); the manager keeps or removes each one with a single icon toggle on the scorecard rows.

## Non-goals

- **Super-over scorecards** — their own spec, [`105`](#rollout-notes). It is not known whether super-over runs and wickets count for statistics, and the answer changes how entries are marked. In 101 every scorecard entry belongs to a `REGULAR` innings; a scorecard entry sent for a `SUPER_OVER` innings is rejected. Super-over innings totals from `100` are untouched.
- **Opposition scorecard** (their batters, bowlers, fielders) — `102`. 101 records the **opposition's innings only as `100`'s totals** plus the extras breakdown and a non-bowler-wicket count, because our bowling figures must reconcile against them. Dismissal detail for opposition batters is `102`.
- **Statistics, averages, career pages** — `103`. 101 only guarantees the rows `103` needs: a stable link to `player_profile_id`, `NULL` for unlinked guests, and which rows may count (see "Guests and borrowed players").
- **Import adapters** — `104`. The canonical `ResultDraft` gains the scorecard section so adapters can fill it; no provider code here.
- **Bowler and fielder of a dismissal on our batters, and per-batter opposition wickets** (decided 2026-10-14): 101 records how a batter was out but not who bowled or caught. There is no free-text Details control and no `bowler_*` / `fielder_*` column in 101; both arrive in `102` with the opposition roster. Opposition wickets not credited to a bowler stay a single number (`non_bowler_wickets`) until `102`.
- **Ball-by-ball detail:** fall of wickets, partnerships, over-by-over, dot balls, minutes batted, wagon wheels, catches dropped. This is not a scoring app (`100`). The outline's `dots` column is **dropped**: no scorecard format prints it.
- **Standout thresholds, a club settings table or a thresholds dialog.** Decided 2026-10-14: no `club_result_settings`. Standouts use relative rules in code (see Standouts), so they work for any format and age group. Per-league, format-aware thresholds are deferred (Rollout Notes).
- **Match graphics** (a broadcast-style scoreboard strip of the top batters and bowler). Future consumer of the featured flags; not specified here.
- **Captain and wicketkeeper flags on the scorecard.** Dropped (decision 2026-10-14); `103` takes keeper dismissals from whatever it can derive, not from a flag here.
- **Creating a `Person` / `PlayerProfile` for a guest** ("create guest profile"; confirmed: no `Person` or `PlayerProfile` is ever created from a scorecard in 101), and **bulk linking** of a guest name across many past matches. Both are tied to the roadmap's player-linking item (cross-club persons, merge, confirm steps) and are deferred; 101 builds single-match linking only.
- **Rotation rules and warnings** ("opened in the last N matches"): only the data they need (actual and planned order) is recorded. The rule itself is a later spec.
- **Per-role capture rights,** player- or public-facing scorecards, notifications on publish: unchanged from `100`'s non-goals.
- **Multi-innings formats:** limited overs only, as in `100`.

## User Stories

- As a club manager, I can open **Our batting** and see my side's players already listed in the announced batting order, so that I only enter what happened.
- As a club manager, I can change the batting order by moving a batter up or down, so that the order I record is the order they actually batted.
- As a club manager, I can swap a selected player for someone else on a single row (a last-minute change) and keep the rest of that row, so that I never retype the figures.
- As a club manager, I can type a name for a guest or borrowed player who is not in the system, so that the scorecard is complete and adds up.
- As a club manager, I am only asked for dismissal details for as many batters as wickets fell, and the remaining batters are marked not out or did not bat for me, so that I do the minimum.
- As a club manager, I can see at a glance when the batters out do not match the wickets in the result, so that I catch a typo before publishing.
- As a club manager, I can enter the extras as wides, no-balls, byes, leg byes and penalty runs, so that the innings total can be checked and bowlers are charged correctly.
- As a club manager, I can enter our bowlers' overs, maidens, runs, wickets, wides and no-balls, pre-listed from the line-up, so that I only fill numbers.
- As a club manager, I can record catches, stumpings and run-outs per fielder, so that fielding is credited.
- As a club manager, I am warned (never blocked) when the scorecard does not agree with the result's totals, and I can acknowledge the warning at publish, so that an honest scorer's mismatch does not stop me.
- As a club manager, I can see suggested standouts marked with an icon on the batting, bowling and fielding rows, and remove or add one for any player with a single tap on that icon, so that the result tells who shone.
- As a club manager, I can name the player of the match (official, from either side, or the club team's).
- As a club manager, I can link a guest row to a registered player afterwards, so that a mistake about who played can be fixed.
- As a coach, I can later see where each player actually batted against where they were planned, so that rotation can be judged (data only in this slice).

## Data Model Changes

Migration sketch: `backend/src/main/resources/db/changelog/v1/044-match-results-our-side-scorecard.sql` (`043-match-results.sql` exists; confirm the next number at plan time). All new tables are scoped through `match_result` (tenancy is `match_result.club_id`; no duplicate `club_id` column on child rows). Enums are `CHECK`-constrained varchars, as in `043`.

**No data-model or API change for the two UI decisions.** Reordering writes the existing `position` (batters) and the existing row order (bowlers) through the whole-scorecard replace; the Full name / First name toggle is display-only and stores nothing.

### Why the player list is its own table: `result_player`

A guest can bat, bowl and field in the same match; a swap, a link to a registered player and a duplicate check are each **one decision about one person**. So the scorecard first records **who played for our side**, once, and the batting, bowling and fielding rows point at that list. Swapping or linking is then one update, every row follows, and "matches played" (for `103`) is a row in this table.

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | uuid pk | |
| `match_result_id` | uuid not null | fk `match_result`, on delete cascade |
| `side` | varchar | `HOME` / `AWAY`; must be a side that is ours (team id set on the match). Both sides are ours in an internal fixture. `102` will add opposition rows to this table with free-text names |
| `player_profile_id` | uuid null | fk `player_profile` (this club's profile; stats roll up by `person_id` through it) |
| `display_name` | varchar null | the name **as written**; required when `player_profile_id` is null (a guest). Kept after linking, as an audit of what was typed |
| `linked_by`, `linked_at` | uuid null, timestamptz null | set by the link route (audit of a link made on a draft or published result) |
| `planned_batting_order` | int null | **snapshot** of `MatchSidePlayer.batting_order` when the line-up was pre-filled; never changed by edits |
| `replaced_player_profile_id` | uuid null | the selected player this person replaced, when swapped ("planned X, Y played") |
| `substitute` | boolean | a substitute fielder who is not one of the eleven; does not count as a match played. **Appearance rule for `103` (decided 2026-10-14):** every non-substitute `result_player` of a published result is a match played, even with no batting, bowling or fielding row |

Constraints: `CHECK (player_profile_id IS NOT NULL OR display_name IS NOT NULL)`; `UNIQUE (match_result_id, side, player_profile_id) WHERE player_profile_id IS NOT NULL` (a registered player appears once per side; in a derby the same person on both sides is allowed by the schema and flagged by `PLAYER_ON_BOTH_SIDES`, a warning, see Derby); `UNIQUE (match_result_id, side, lower(display_name)) WHERE player_profile_id IS NULL` (two unlinked guests on one side must be told apart: "John", "John (Dave's friend)"; the rule is **per side**, so the same guest name may appear once on each side of a derby).

### `match_innings` (altered; `043` fixed the base columns)

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `wides`, `no_balls`, `byes`, `leg_byes`, `penalty_runs` | int null | the extras breakdown, **in runs** (a wide that runs four is 5 wides), ≥ 0. The batting side's extras |
| `extras` | int null | existing column, now the **stored total**. When any component is non-null the server **derives** `extras` as their sum; a client total that disagrees with the components is a save error (`EXTRAS_TOTAL_MISMATCH`). With no components (a summary-level result) `extras` is whatever was sent, as in `100` |
| `non_bowler_wickets` | int null | wickets in this innings not credited to a bowler (run-outs, obstructing the field, handled ball, hit ball twice, timed out, retired out). Needed to reconcile our bowlers' wickets with the innings wickets without opposition batter rows. It stays a single number until `102` supplies per-batter dismissals (decided 2026-10-14). When the innings is one of **ours batting**, it is derived from the batters' dismissals instead (every wicket `how_out` that is not bowler-credited, which includes `RETIRED_OUT`) and this column is ignored |
| (constraint) | | `ux_match_innings_sequence` becomes `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`, so innings can be resequenced in place (see below) |

**Who is charged (cricket rules, enforced as the meaning of the columns):** wides and no-balls are charged to the bowler (they appear in the bowler's `runs`, and optionally in their `wides` / `no_balls`); byes, leg byes and penalty runs are **not** charged to any bowler. Runs off the bat from a no-ball are charged to the bowler and credited to the batter. Therefore, for an innings: `bowlers' runs + byes + leg byes + penalty runs = innings runs`, and `batters' runs + extras = innings runs`.

### Innings identity must survive saves (a change to `100`'s upsert)

`100`'s `PUT .../result` deletes and recreates the innings on every save. Scorecard entries cascade from `match_innings`, so that would silently erase the scorecard every time the Scores step is re-saved. 101 therefore changes the upsert to **reconcile innings in place**, matched on `(kind, batting_side)` (limited overs: one regular innings per side), updating `sequence`, runs, wickets, balls, `all_out` and the extras columns on the existing row and keeping its `id`. A changed toss re-orders (resequences) the innings without touching their entries, which belong to a side. An innings removed from the body is deleted with its entries; the wizard asks first when entries exist (`ConfirmDialog`: "This removes the scorecard for that innings"). This is the only change to `100`'s behaviour and it is invisible when no scorecard exists.

### `batting_entry` (our batters; one row per player in the innings, including those who did not bat)

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | uuid pk | |
| `match_innings_id` | uuid not null | fk, on delete cascade; the innings must be a `REGULAR` innings **in which `result_player.side` batted** |
| `result_player_id` | uuid not null | fk `result_player`, on delete cascade; **unique with `match_innings_id`** |
| `position` | int null | **the actual batting order**, 1-based and distinct per innings; `NULL` for `DID_NOT_BAT`. Planned order stays on `result_player.planned_batting_order` |
| `how_out` | varchar | `DismissalType` (below), including `NOT_OUT` and `DID_NOT_BAT` |
| `runs`, `balls`, `fours`, `sixes` | int null | `runs` required once the row batted (validated at publish); balls, fours and sixes are optional and `NULL` means "not on the scorecard", never 0 |
| `featured_override`, `featured` | varchar null, boolean null | standout marking, see "Standouts" (the same two columns exist on `bowling_entry` and `fielding_entry`) |

**No bowler or fielder columns in 101** (decided 2026-10-14): `how_out` is the whole dismissal. `102` adds the dismissing bowler and fielder (a `result_player` reference or free text) to this table together with the opposition roster.

**`DismissalType` enum:** `NOT_OUT`, `DID_NOT_BAT`, `BOWLED`, `CAUGHT`, `CAUGHT_AND_BOWLED`, `LBW`, `STUMPED`, `RUN_OUT`, `HIT_WICKET`, `HANDLED_BALL`, `OBSTRUCTING_FIELD`, `HIT_BALL_TWICE`, `TIMED_OUT`, `RETIRED_NOT_OUT`, `RETIRED_OUT`. A caught-behind is `CAUGHT`. Bowler-credited dismissals: `BOWLED`, `CAUGHT`, `CAUGHT_AND_BOWLED`, `LBW`, `STUMPED`, `HIT_WICKET`. A **wicket** (counts toward the innings wicket count) is every value except `NOT_OUT`, `DID_NOT_BAT` and `RETIRED_NOT_OUT`. A wicket that is not bowler-credited (`RUN_OUT`, `HANDLED_BALL`, `OBSTRUCTING_FIELD`, `HIT_BALL_TWICE`, `TIMED_OUT`, `RETIRED_OUT`) is a **non-bowler wicket** for bowling reconciliation.

**Retirement rules (decided 2026-10-14, adopted as written):**

| Kind | When | May return? | Recorded as | Wicket / dismissal? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retired not out (retired hurt: injury, illness or other unavoidable cause) | Unavoidable cause | Yes, with umpire consent, at the fall of a wicket or another retirement | `RETIRED_NOT_OUT` | **No**, not a wicket or dismissal. A returning batter is **one row** |
| Retired out (tactical or voluntary exit for any other reason) | Any other reason | No, unless the opposing captain gives permission | `RETIRED_OUT` | **Yes**, counts for the innings wicket count, but **no bowler is credited**, so it is a non-bowler wicket like a run-out for bowling reconciliation |

Edge case, not specially built: a `RETIRED_OUT` batter permitted back by the opposing captain is a **new row** (the manager adds the player again as a separate entry). This is rare and is documented only; there is no dedicated control, and the one-row-per-player rule applies to `RETIRED_NOT_OUT` returns.

### Standouts: relative suggestions, a per-entry override, frozen at publish

Decided 2026-10-14: a threshold such as "30 runs" cannot be a single club-wide number, because it depends on the format and the age group (T10 against T50, under 9s against a first team). So there is **no `club_result_settings` table and no thresholds dialog**. Standouts are **suggestions**, computed on read, in code, by rules that are **relative to the innings and independent of format**. The constants live in one place (`StandoutRules`, one class, unit tested): `TOP_N = 3`, `BATTING_SHARE = 0.25`, `ECONOMY_FACTOR = 0.75`, `MIN_BALLS_FOR_ECONOMY = 12`, `FIELDING_MIN = 2`.

- **Batting** (per innings, our batters): rank by runs descending, then fewer balls. A batter is suggested when they are in the top `TOP_N` **and** their runs are at least `BATTING_SHARE` of the innings' batting total (the innings runs), **or** they are the top scorer of the innings with at least one run (so a short innings or a low-scoring side still has a standout).
- **Bowling** (per innings, our bowlers): rank by wickets descending, then lower economy (runs per over, from balls). Suggested when in the top `TOP_N` **and** (at least 1 wicket, **or** at least `MIN_BALLS_FOR_ECONOMY` balls bowled with an economy at or below `ECONOMY_FACTOR` of the innings run rate, runs per over of that innings).
- **Fielding** (per innings): rank by catches + stumpings + run-outs descending; suggested when in the top `TOP_N` and at least `FIELDING_MIN` dismissals.
- Guest rows qualify (they performed) and show the Guest tag.

**These are suggestions, not facts.** The manager can add or remove a standout for any player with a **simple icon toggle on the scorecard rows** (a star next to the player in the batting, bowling and fielding tables and on the Awards step, like the icon next to a player in the legacy app that the user can remove). A tap on a suggested row removes it; a tap on any other row adds it. Nothing is ever forced.

**Where it is stored (per entry, computed and edited apart, so recomputing never erases an edit):**
- `featured_override` (varchar null on `batting_entry`, `bowling_entry`, `fielding_entry`): `FEATURE` (manager added), `HIDE` (manager removed a suggestion) or `NULL` (no edit). Written only by the manager's toggle, through the scorecard `PUT`. The server never overwrites it when the scorecard is recomputed or re-saved.
- The **computed** suggestion is never stored on a draft; it is derived on every read from the scorecard and the rules.
- The **effective** value is `FEATURE` => featured, `HIDE` => not featured, `NULL` => the computed suggestion. It is returned on every entry in the scorecard `GET` as `suggested`, `featuredOverride` and `featured`.
- **Frozen on publish:** `featured` (boolean null) is `NULL` on a draft and set at publish to the effective value; a published result reads `featured` as stored, so a later change to the rules (or a link) never rewrites history. Unpublish clears `featured` back to `NULL` (overrides are kept). No snapshot column on `match_result`.
- **Future use:** dynamic match graphics (a scoreboard-style strip like a broadcast graphic, showing the top batters and the top bowler) will read the featured flags. That feature is not specified here; the flags are the contract it will use.
- **Deferred:** per-league or per-format-aware thresholds (league playing conditions already give format and overs) are a later refinement of the same computation; see Rollout Notes and `docs/roadmap.md`.

### `match_result` (altered)

`detail_level` is now set by the server: `OUR_SIDE` when the result has a scorecard (at least one `result_player`), else `SUMMARY`. `FULL` stays `102`'s. No other column changes.

### Guests and borrowed players: the recommendation

A guest or borrowed player is a `result_player` row with `player_profile_id = NULL` and a `display_name`. Decisions:

1. **Free-text row, no automatic profile.** Nothing creates a `Person` or `PlayerProfile` behind the manager's back (a profile would appear in rosters, verification statuses (`040`) and the player list, and risks the silent duplicate persons the roadmap's link item exists to fix).
2. **Borrowed from another team of our own club** is not a guest: the swap picker offers "Anyone in the club" (default scope is this team's squad for the match's season), and the row is a real, linked player. Selecting someone outside the squad shows a warning (`PLAYER_NOT_IN_SQUAD`), not a block.
3. **What an unlinked row counts for:** it **is** part of the scorecard, the innings, the team's totals and every reconciliation check (the runs were scored). It **does not feed any player's statistics** (`103` aggregates by `person_id` through `player_profile_id`; no link, no person), and it is not a "match played" for anyone. It can win a standout or the player of the match; both then show the "Guest" tag, and neither counts toward a person's awards until linked.
4. **Linking later** (`POST .../result/players/{id}/link`) sets `player_profile_id` on that one row; every batting, bowling, fielding, standout and award row follows because they point at `result_player`. `display_name` is kept as written. Linking is allowed on a published result (decided 2026-10-14) as the **only edit allowed on a published result**, because it changes no figure on the scorecard, only who is credited. It is **audited**: `updated_by`/`updated_at` on the `result_player` row, plus `linked_by`, `linked_at` columns (nullable) on `result_player` recording who linked and when.
5. **Duplicate-name risk:** (a) within a match the partial unique index stops two identical guest names on a side; (b) a guest whose name matches a registered club player (case-insensitive, trimmed) raises `GUEST_NAME_MATCHES_PLAYER` offering **Use this player** (one tap converts the row to a linked player); (c) the name field offers previously used guest names for the club (`results/guest-names`), so the same visitor is spelled once; (d) the same name in different matches is **not** assumed to be the same person: only the manager's explicit link says so. Bulk linking of "John" across past matches is deferred (Non-goals) and would need `097`'s preview-and-confirm.
6. **A registered player at another club** is, to this club, a guest until the roadmap's cross-club link exists. When it does, linking a row to a person known at another club is part of that spec, not this one.

### Derby (internal fixture, both sides ours)

A derby is a match where both `MatchSide`s have a team of this club. Decided 2026-10-14:

1. **Each side is its own scorecard.** Home and Away each have their own `result_player` list, batting, bowling and fielding entries, and their own extras and non-bowler-wicket numbers. Nothing is shared or mirrored: the home side's batting is reconciled against the away side's bowling by the same rules as any match, once per side. Each side's list pre-fills from **that team's own** `MatchSide` / `MatchSidePlayer`, via `scorecard/prefill` (one list per ours side).
2. **Awards in a derby:** exactly **one official** `PLAYER_OF_MATCH` per result (a player from either side's list, or free text) and **one optional** `TEAM_PLAYER_OF_MATCH` per ours side, so up to two (one per team). In a normal match with one ours side it is one official and at most one team award, as before.
3. **Guest names are unique per side, not per match.** The same guest name on both sides is **not** an error: it raises the info issue `GUEST_ON_BOTH_SIDES` ("also on <other team>"), because two different visitors can share a name and a guest may genuinely play for one team then the other. Nothing blocks or needs acknowledging.
4. **A registered player on both sides is a warning, not an error** (`PLAYER_ON_BOTH_SIDES`, acknowledgeable at publish). A player can be borrowed between teams of the same club in a derby (a team short of players), so it can be true, but it is rare enough to be worth a check. The message names the player and both teams. This **replaces** the earlier error "the same registered player on both sides" and relaxes the `result_player` uniqueness to per side (above).
5. **Statistics (note for `103`):** a player who appears for both sides of a derby counts **one** match played, not two (the appearance rule counts the match once per person). Their batting, bowling and fielding rows from both sides still count in full, since the figures are real. `103` must aggregate matches played by distinct match per `person_id`, not by `result_player` row.

## API Contract

Same base, access rules and section narrowing as `100` (`@PreAuthorize("@access.canAccessClub(authentication, #clubId)")`, writes through the existing per-match administrable check; any club manager). All routes below are under `/api/v1/manage/clubs/{clubId}`. Draft-only rules and the 409s of `100` apply unchanged.

| Endpoint | Access | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /matches/{matchId}/result/scorecard/prefill` | club manager | The proposed line-up per ours side (two lists in a derby, each from that team's own selection) from `MatchSide` / `MatchSidePlayer`: players in batting order (those with a null order after those with one), role, 12th man listed as an available substitute, plus `plannedBattingOrder`. Works with or without a result; writes nothing. Empty lists (not an error) when no team was selected |
| `GET /matches/{matchId}/result/scorecard` | club manager | The saved scorecard: `players`, per-innings `extras` breakdown and `nonBowlerWickets`, `batting`, `bowling`, `fielding`, `awards`. Every batting, bowling and fielding entry carries `suggested`, `featuredOverride` and `featured` (see Standouts). 404 if there is no result; an empty scorecard if the result has none |
| `PUT /matches/{matchId}/result` (existing) | club manager | `ResultDraft` gains an optional `scorecard` section with the same shape (including each entry's `featuredOverride`, written by the standout toggle). Absent/null = scorecard untouched; present = the whole draft scorecard is replaced in one transaction (players keep their `id` when sent, new rows carry a `clientKey`). Save enforces **structural** validity only (see rules); innings are reconciled in place (see Data Model) |
| `POST /matches/{matchId}/result/validate` (existing) | club manager | Accepts the same `ResultDraft` including the scorecard, so the wizard can check before saving; the scorecard rules below run here and at publish; issues now carry `code`, `step` and `side` (see below). In a derby the response lets the client group issues per side and the saved scorecard read reports each ours side's scorecard status (`NONE` / `PARTIAL` / `COMPLETE`) |
| `POST /matches/{matchId}/result/publish` / `unpublish` (existing) | club manager | Publish re-validates the scorecard, requires the acknowledged warnings, and freezes each entry's `featured`; unpublish clears `featured` (overrides kept) |
| `POST /matches/{matchId}/result/players/{resultPlayerId}/link` | club manager | Link an unlinked `result_player` to a `player_profile_id` of this club. Allowed on a draft or a published result (the only write allowed on a published result; audited with `linked_by`/`linked_at`); 400 if the profile is already in the result or belongs to another club |
| `GET /results/guest-names?teamId=&q=` | club manager | Distinct guest `display_name`s used by the club (most recent first, with a count), for the name field's suggestions. Paged, as in `backend.md` |
| `GET /matches/{matchId}/result` (existing) | club manager | Gains `hasScorecard`; `detailLevel` is `OUR_SIDE` when there is one |
| `GET /results` list (existing) | club manager | `ResultListItemDto` gains `playerOfTheMatch` (display name or null) for the card |

**Save-time (structural) rules, errors on `PUT`:** all counts ≥ 0; `position` distinct and ≥ 1; `how_out` valid for the table; a row's `result_player` belongs to this result and its `side` matches the innings (batting entries on an innings that side batted; bowling and fielding on an innings the opposing side batted); one batting, bowling and fielding row per player per innings; `result_player` has a profile or a name, a profile belongs to this club, a profile appears once per side (on both sides is allowed by the schema and is the `PLAYER_ON_BOTH_SIDES` warning); no entry on a `SUPER_OVER` innings (`SCORECARD_SUPER_OVER_NOT_SUPPORTED`, pointing at `105`); an extras total that disagrees with its components; a scorecard on a result with no innings for that side. A draft may otherwise be incomplete or inconsistent, as in `100`.

**Validation (`ResultValidator`, shared with imports; run by validate and publish).** Errors block publish; warnings are acknowledged at publish with `acknowledgedWarnings`, exactly `100`'s mechanism. None run for an innings that lacks runs or balls, nor for `FORFEIT`/`ABANDONED`/`NO_RESULT`; only regular innings are considered.

*Errors (logically impossible):* a batting row that batted (`how_out` not `DID_NOT_BAT`) with no `runs`; `fours × 4 + sixes × 6` exceeds `runs`; a `DID_NOT_BAT` row with figures; maidens above complete overs bowled; two players claiming the same batting `position`; official player of the match missing both player and name; more than one official player of the match, or more than one team player of the match for a side. (The same registered player on both sides of a derby is no longer an error: see the `PLAYER_ON_BOTH_SIDES` warning.)

*Warnings (reconciliation; the codes name the rules in code and tests):*

| Code | Rule |
|---|---|
| `BATTERS_OUT_NOT_WICKETS` | Our batting innings: the count of batters with a wicket `how_out` (including `RETIRED_OUT`, excluding `RETIRED_NOT_OUT`) ≠ the innings wickets |
| `NOT_OUT_COUNT` | More than two batters `NOT_OUT` when the innings is not all out, more than one when all out |
| `BATTING_TOTAL_MISMATCH` | Σ batters' runs + extras ≠ innings runs (the message gives both sides of the sum) |
| `BOWLER_BALLS_MISMATCH` | Σ our bowlers' balls ≠ the opposition innings' balls |
| `BOWLING_RUNS_MISMATCH` | Σ our bowlers' runs + byes + leg byes + penalty runs ≠ the opposition innings runs. With no extras breakdown on that innings, only Σ bowlers' runs ≤ innings runs is checked |
| `BOWLER_WICKETS_MISMATCH` | Σ our bowlers' wickets + `non_bowler_wickets` (which includes opposition retired-out batters) ≠ the opposition innings wickets |
| `FIELDING_EXCEEDS_WICKETS` | Σ catches + stumpings + run-outs credited > the wickets in that innings, or credited run-outs > `non_bowler_wickets` |
| `WIDES_MISMATCH`, `NO_BALLS_MISMATCH` | Σ bowlers' wides (no-balls) differs from the innings' wides (no-balls) when both are entered |
| `BOWLER_OVERS_EXCEED_LIMIT` | A bowler's balls exceed the league's maximum overs per bowler (`LeaguePlayingConditions.maxOversPerBowler`, converted to balls as `100` does for innings) |
| `LINEUP_TOO_LARGE` | More than eleven non-substitute players for a side |
| `PLAYER_NOT_IN_SQUAD` | A linked player is not in that team's squad for the match's season (`TeamSquadMember`), the same test `029` applies to selection |
| `GUEST_NAME_MATCHES_PLAYER` | An unlinked name equals a registered club player's name |
| `PLAYER_ON_BOTH_SIDES` | Derby only: the same registered player is on both sides' lists. Acknowledgeable (a player can be borrowed between teams of the same club) |

*Info (never blocks, nothing to acknowledge):* `GUEST_ON_BOTH_SIDES` (derby only): the same guest name (case-insensitive, trimmed) is on both sides, shown as "also on <other team>".

**Validation issues gain `code` and `step` (decided 2026-10-14).** Every validation issue, errors and warnings alike, becomes an object that carries an optional `code`, an optional `step` (the wizard step to jump to, for example `SCORES`, `OUR_BATTING`, `OUR_BOWLING`, `AWARDS`) and an optional `side` (`HOME` / `AWAY`: the side of the scorecard the issue belongs to, set on every scorecard issue and left null for match-level issues, so the Review step can say "Home batting" or "Away bowling" and **Fix** can open the right side's tab), alongside the existing message. Info issues use a `severity` of `INFO` beside errors and warnings, in the same shape. This is **additive** in OpenAPI (existing `errors` and `warnings` message strings stay for current callers) and **amends `100`'s "message strings only"**. The codes come from the existing backend `ResultValidator.WarningCode` enum (`WINNER_NOT_HIGHER_SCORER`, `MARGIN_MISMATCH`, `TIE_NOT_LEVEL`, `SUPER_OVER_WINNER_MISMATCH`, `DLS_WINNER_MISMATCH`, each already tagged in code), which 101 **extends** with the scorecard codes in the table above; errors get a parallel `ErrorCode` enum in the same class. This **resolves the roadmap item "stable warning codes" for results** and lets the UI **drop message-text deduplication** (`ReviewStep`'s `dedupeWarnings`, which matches on wording) in favour of `code`; removing it is a follow-up once the codes are served, not a blocker.

**Exceptions:** none new beyond reusing `InvalidResultException` (structured `errors`/`warnings`) and `ResultPublishedException`. The link route raises `PlayerAlreadyInResultException` (extends `ConflictException`, named per `backend.md`). `OpenAPI`: `backend/openapi/openapi.yaml` regenerated by hand per the repo's practice, additions only (the new `scorecard` section is optional on an existing body).

## UI Requirements

Match the existing Results wizard and the gold-standard patterns: `RecordFormScreen`, `FormSectionHeading`, `InfoCard`, `KeyFigureTile`, `CompactToggleGroup`, `ConfirmDialog`, `BottomSheet`, `CardProgressBar`, `OversInput` and `oversFormat` from `100`, zebra rows via `zebraTint`, `badgeSx` tones. Mobile-first at 375 px; the poll Responses page is the model for dense rows that stay usable on a phone. Per the repo's rule, a mockup of the grid on a phone and a desktop is approved before the build; none is included here (mockups: https://claude.ai/artifact/2QZZKDccNRVoJ7TeyHsjyT, whose new **Derby variants** board shows the Home | Away switch, the two team awards and the per-side Review status). Two UI decisions made after the first draft are recorded below: reordering follows the Team Selection pattern, and a Full name / First name display toggle.

### Wizard steps

`RESULT_STEPS` (`ResultCapturePage.tsx`) is data-driven; 101 **registers three entries between Scores and Review**, each with an `isApplicable(draft)` predicate (shown only when at least one innings with runs exists, hidden for forfeit, abandoned and no result) and flagged **Optional** in the stepper (a result may be published at summary level; "Skip scorecard" goes straight to Review). The end of the Scores step gains a quiet prompt: **Add our scorecard** (continue) or **Skip to review**. Autosave on step change and **Save draft** behave as in `100`. Order after this slice: Result, Toss and field, Scores, **Our batting**, **Our bowling and fielding**, **Awards**, Review. *(Assumed, to confirm:)* if the stepper is crowded on a phone, the three optional steps are grouped visually (one "Scorecard (optional)" bracket around them); the steps themselves and their order do not change.

The **name display toggle** (next section) sits at the top of every scorecard step, the Review step and the result page.

An internal fixture (a derby, both teams ours) shows a **Home | Away** switch (`CompactToggleGroup`, as the Select team page) at the top of **every** scorecard step (Our batting, Our bowling and fielding, Awards), labelled with each team's name. Each side is entered separately and pre-filled from **that team's own** selection; the switch changes which side's list, innings and strips are shown, and the draft keeps both. A small dot on a tab marks a side with unsaved or incomplete entries. A single-ours-side match shows no switch. See `Derby` in the data model for the rules.

### Player name display toggle (Full name / First name)

A small `CompactToggleGroup` at the top of the three scorecard steps (Our batting, Our bowling and fielding, Awards), the Review step and the result page: **Full name** | **First name**. Junior coaches often do not know players' surnames, so first-name mode lets them work from what they know.

- **First-name mode:** the plain first name. Where two players *visible in the same view* share a first name, a short surname tag is added: the first letter of the surname ("Sam V"); for particle surnames an abbreviation: the particles' initials in lower case plus the first letter of the main surname ("Sam vdW" for van der Westhuizen). If two tags still collide, the tag lengthens one letter at a time ("Sam Va", "Sam Vi"); as a last resort the full surname is shown.
- **Display-only.** Nothing is stored on the server and no API field changes. **Guests follow the same rules as registered players (decided 2026-10-14).** A guest's typed free-text name is split into first name and surname the same way (first word is the first name, the rest is the surname, with the same particle handling), and the same collision rule applies across guests and registered players together: two players named Sam, guest or registered, get the short surname tag ("Sam V"). A guest typed as a single word has no surname to tag, so the tag logic falls back to the full typed name and the existing duplicate warning (the within-match unique guest name rule and `GUEST_NAME_MATCHES_PLAYER`) does the telling apart.
- **One pure helper**, `playerDisplayName` in `ui/src/utils/` (takes the names in the current view, the mode and a player, returns the label; no React), and **one shared `PlayerName` component** used by every scorecard row, the picker's current row, awards, standouts and every result-page line (so no screen formats a name itself). The accessible names ("Runs, A. Smith") use the same label.
- **Remembered per user in browser storage**, in the manner of `useListViewPreference` (a small hook, try/catch around storage, default **Full name**). Not shared between users or devices.
- *Later:* it could become a club or section default setting; out of scope here.

### Standout icon toggle (all scorecard rows)

Every batting, bowling and fielding row, on the entry steps, the Awards step and the result page, shows a **star icon button** (`StandoutToggle`, small, 44 px target on a phone, accessible name "Featured, A. Smith"). Filled = featured (effective), outlined = not. Tapping it writes `featuredOverride` (`HIDE` on a suggested row, `FEATURE` on any other; tapping again returns the override to `NULL` when it matches the suggestion). A suggested-but-not-edited star is tinted as "suggested" so the manager can tell it from one they set. On a published result the stars are read-only. Icons under 32 px, so MUI `@mui/icons-material` (`Star`, `StarBorder`).

### Step: Our batting

1. **Pre-fill.** On first open the step calls `scorecard/prefill` and builds the grid locally (nothing is written until a save): rows in the planned batting order, then players without an order, as did-not-bat. A short strip shows where it came from ("From the announced team") with **Reset to selection** (`ConfirmDialog`; adds missing planned players and re-snapshots the planned order, never silently removes entered rows). With no selection, the grid starts empty and the add control is open.
2. **Wicket-driven form.** The innings' **wickets and balls come from the Scores step** and are shown in a meter above the grid (`CardProgressBar`, "Out 4 of 6"). The grid starts with the first `wickets + 2` players as batted (the two at the crease at the end; fewer if the line-up is shorter) and the rest as **Did not bat**. As the manager marks how each batter was out, once the number of dismissals equals the wickets the remaining batted rows become **Not out** automatically (and a row cleared reverts them to undecided); the manager is asked only for dismissals up to the wicket count. Wickets 10 means all out: ten dismissals and the last batter not out (or the eleventh absent). A `RETIRED_OUT` row counts as a dismissal for the meter and the auto-fill; a `RETIRED_NOT_OUT` row does not, and a batter who retired hurt and returned is still one row with their final figures. If the manager marks more dismissals than wickets, the meter turns amber with "1 more than the result says" and `BATTERS_OUT_NOT_WICKETS` shows on review; nothing is forced.
3. **The grid.** Columns: `#`, Player, How out, R, B, 4s, 6s. There is no bowler or fielder control in 101: **How out** is the whole dismissal (`102` adds them). Beside the grid, a totals strip reads **Total 148 = 135 off the bat + 13 extras ✓** (or an amber mismatch, `BATTING_TOTAL_MISMATCH`, never a block), and an **Extras** row holds Wides, No-balls, Byes, Leg byes, Penalty, with the sum shown and written to `extras`.
4. **Keyboard-first.** Tab/Shift+Tab and Enter/Shift+Enter move along a row and wrap to the next row's first number cell; Arrow Up/Down move within a column; number cells select their content on focus and use `inputMode="numeric"`; **How out** is a type-ahead (type `c` Caught, `b` Bowled, `l` LBW, `r` Run out, `s` Stumped, `n` Not out, `d` Did not bat; `cb` Caught and bowled; `ro` Retired out, `rn` Retired not out; Enter accepts), so a scorer never touches the mouse; `/` opens the player picker on the row; `?` lists the shortcuts. Focus is visible; every cell has an accessible name ("Runs, A. Smith"); the grid is real inputs in a table, not a custom widget.
5. **Phone.** Each batter is a compact card: name, a How-out chip row, and R / B / 4s / 6s steppers on a numeric keypad; the same order and the same auto-marking. There is no dragging on a phone: tapping the name opens the name popup (item 6), whose Move up / Move down / Batting position are the reorder route, 44 px targets.
6. **Reordering and swapping: the Team Selection pattern.** Reordering follows `ui/src/components/TeamSelectionList/TeamSelectionList.tsx`: on desktop, native HTML5 drag and drop with a drag handle on each row; on any device (and the only route on a phone and for the keyboard), **tapping the player's name opens a popup menu** with **Move up**, **Move down** and a **Batting position** number field (type 3 and the row moves to 3). The same name popup holds **Swap player...** (opens the `PlayerSlotPicker` below and replaces *who* the row is, keeping runs, balls and how-out; the swapped-out selected player is remembered in `replaced_player_profile_id` and shown as "replaced A. Smith"), **Link to a registered player...** (guest rows only) and **Remove** (deletes the row after confirm). *(Assumed, to confirm:)* the popup offers only Swap player, with no "Mark as guest" entry. Moving a row changes `position`; `plannedBattingOrder` is untouched, so the row can show "planned 3". **Add player** appends one. There is no Alt+Arrow shortcut. Player names in every row are rendered through `PlayerName` (name display toggle above).
7. **`PlayerSlotPicker`** (new, shared by every step): a searchable list with three scopes, **This team's squad** (default, this season; the 12th man and unselected squad members first), **Anyone in the club**, and **Guest (name only)** with the club's previous guest names as suggestions. Players already on the list are shown disabled with the reason. Choosing a guest whose name matches a registered player offers **Use this player**. A guest row carries a visible "Guest" chip everywhere (grid, awards, result page).

### Step: Our bowling and fielding

For each innings in which the opposition batted (one panel per innings in an internal fixture, one per side): a **Bowling** table pre-listing the players whose selected role is bowler or all-rounder (and anyone added), columns Bowler, O (`OversInput`, "8.2"), M, R, W, with Wd and Nb behind a **More** control; a **Bowled so far** strip beside it ("Balls 238 of 240 · 2 left", "Runs 131 of 140 conceded incl. 6 extras not charged"); and the **Opposition extras** row: Wides, No-balls, Byes, Leg byes, Penalty (with the one-line note "Byes, leg byes and penalty runs are not charged to a bowler"), plus **Wickets not credited to a bowler** (run-outs and similar), a single number that closes the wickets reconciliation ("5 + 1 = 6 ✓"; it stays one number until `102`, and an opposition retired-out batter is counted in it). A **Fielding** list underneath shows the line-up with three tap-to-increment counters (Catches, Stumpings, Run-outs); only players with a credit are saved. Keyboard behaviour, phone cards, swap, add and the Guest chip are those of Our batting. Bowling order is the row order, reordered exactly as batters are (drag handle on desktop; name popup with Move up, Move down, Bowling position, Swap player..., Link to a registered player... and Remove).

### Step: Awards

Three blocks, as `InfoCard`s in the poll-page style:
1. **Standouts:** the effective list grouped Batting, Bowling, Fielding, each row the player (Guest chip when unlinked) with figures ("62 off 48", "3 for 21 (8.0)", "2 catches") and the **star toggle**, and no separate Add standout and no thresholds. A note reads "Suggested from the scorecard. Tap the star to remove or add one."
2. **Player of the match (official):** one pick from our list (with standouts first), or **Someone from the other side** with a name field (labelled with the opposition's team name), or **None**. In a derby this block lists **both** sides' players (grouped by team) plus free text; there is exactly one official pick.
3. **Our team's player of the match:** one pick from our list per ours side, or none. In a derby this block shows **one pick for each team** (two in all, each optional), picked from that team's own list; the Home | Away switch does not hide either pick on this step (both team awards are shown together).

### Review step and result page

Review shows a scorecard line ("Our batting, bowling and fielding entered" or "Summary only"; in a derby **one line per side**, each with its status `None` / `Partial` / `Complete`, for example "Riverside 1st XI: complete; Riverside 2nd XI: partial"), merges the scorecard warnings into the existing single **Checks** list (each with **Fix** jumping to its step and row via the new `step`; in a derby each issue is prefixed with its side's team name via `side`, and **Fix** opens that side's tab; `GUEST_ON_BOTH_SIDES` shows as a quiet info line), and the card preview gains the player of the match. The result page (`ResultDetailPage`) gains an **Our scorecard** section per innings: batting, bowling and fielding as zebra tables (the whole section scrolls inside its card on a phone, one reduced column set), the extras line, the standouts and the awards; the batting table shows "planned 3" only where it differs, as a small caption under the name *(assumed, to confirm)*. Every player label on the page goes through `PlayerName`, with the Full name / First name toggle at the top of the page. The Review step carries the same toggle (decided 2026-10-14), so the manager can check the scorecard lines, checks list and card preview in whichever mode they entered the figures; it is the same remembered preference, not a separate setting, and every player label on Review goes through `PlayerName`. Edit remains disabled on a published result ("Unpublish to edit"); **Link to player** on a Guest row is the single allowed action on a published result.

### New shared pieces (four-file anatomy each)

`ScorecardGrid` (the keyboard-first row/column engine, generic so `102` reuses it for opposition entries), `PlayerSlotPicker`, `PlayerName` (with the pure helper `playerDisplayName` in `ui/src/utils/` and a `usePlayerNameMode` preference hook), `HowOutSelect`, `ReconcileStrip` (the "Total 148 = 135 + 13 ✓" and "Out 4 of 6" strips; wraps `CardProgressBar`), `ExtrasFields`. **Plan note:** the reorder behaviour (drag handle, drag and drop, the name popup with Move up / Move down / position field) must be **reused or extracted from `TeamSelectionList`** into shared pieces rather than re-implemented a second time; the plan decides the extraction (for example a shared reorderable-row hook plus a row-menu component) and keeps `TeamSelectionList` behaving unchanged. Pure helpers with unit tests: `scorecardPrefill.ts` (selection to rows), `wicketFill.ts` (the auto not-out/did-not-bat rule), `battingTotals.ts`. Dismissal types and their bowler-credited/wicket flags live in one `utils/dismissal.ts` mirrored by the backend enum. The frontend never recomputes standouts or reconciliation as business rules; strips are conveniences, the server's validation is authoritative.

- Batters and bowlers can be reordered by drag and drop with a handle on desktop and, on any device, from the name popup (Move up, Move down, Batting position number); the popup also holds Swap player..., Link to a registered player... (guest rows) and Remove. No dragging is needed on a phone, and no Alt+Arrow shortcut exists.
- A Full name / First name toggle at the top of the scorecard steps, the Review step and the result page changes every player label (rows, awards, standouts, review lines, result page lines) through one `PlayerName` component; first-name mode adds a short surname tag only where two players in the same view share a first name; nothing is stored on the server; the choice is remembered per user in browser storage and defaults to Full name. Guests follow the same rules as registered players: a guest's typed name is split into first name and surname the same way, a guest and a registered player (or two guests) both named Sam get the short surname tag, and a single-word guest falls back to the full typed name and the duplicate warning.
- A swap on one row replaces the player and keeps that row's figures; a swapped-out selected player is shown as "replaced".
- A row can hold a free-text name; it is tagged Guest; it counts in the scorecard totals and checks but is documented and tested as excluded from any player's statistics until linked; two identical guest names on a side are refused; a guest name equal to a registered player's offers **Use this player**.
- With wickets = 6 in the result, marking six batters out leaves the rest of the batted rows not out and the remaining line-up did-not-bat without further input; marking seven shows the over-count and, on review, the `BATTERS_OUT_NOT_WICKETS` warning. A `RETIRED_OUT` batter counts as one of the wickets (and as a non-bowler wicket for bowling reconciliation); a `RETIRED_NOT_OUT` batter does not, and a returning retired-not-out batter is a single row.
- Our batters carry no bowler or fielder in 101 (no column, API field or control); opposition wickets not credited to a bowler are one number, `non_bowler_wickets`.
- Every non-substitute `result_player` of a published result is recorded as a match played for `103`, even with no batting, bowling or fielding row; a substitute is not.
- The batters' runs plus extras are compared with the innings total; our bowlers' balls, runs (plus byes, leg byes and penalty runs), wickets (plus non-bowler wickets) and the fielding credits are compared with the opposition innings; each mismatch is a warning, acknowledged at publish, enforced by the shared `ResultValidator` (a unit test drives it with no wizard involved).
- Extras are stored as wides, no-balls, byes, leg byes and penalty runs; `match_innings.extras` always equals their sum; wides and no-balls are in bowlers' runs and byes, leg byes and penalty are not.
- Re-saving the Scores step, or changing the toss, never deletes scorecard entries.
- A full innings can be entered from the keyboard alone on desktop, and on a 375 px viewport with no horizontal page scroll.
- Standouts are suggested by relative rules in code (top N by runs, wickets then economy, or dismissals, and a share of the team total or the top scorer), with no per-club thresholds and the same rules for any format or age group. A star toggle on every scorecard row removes a suggestion or adds any player; the override is stored per entry apart from the computed value, so recomputing after a scorecard edit keeps it; a published result's featured flags are frozen and do not change when the rules change.
- An official player of the match (from our list or free text for the other side) and the club team's player of the match can be saved; at most one official award per result.
- **Derby:** every scorecard step has a Home | Away switch and each side is entered separately, pre-filled from that team's own selection; Awards allows one official player of the match (from either side's list or free text) and one optional team player of the match per ours side (two in a derby); Review shows each side's scorecard status and every check says which side it belongs to (`side` on the issue, additive); the same guest name on both sides is an info issue ("also on <other team>"), not an error, while two identical guest names on one side are still refused; a registered player on both sides is an acknowledgeable warning (`PLAYER_ON_BOTH_SIDES`), not an error; a player on both sides counts one match played for `103`.
- A scorecard entry for a super-over innings is rejected, and super-over innings totals from `100` behave as before.
- A guest row can be linked to a registered player, including on a published result (the only edit allowed there, audited with who and when), and every row of that guest follows.
- Validation errors and warnings carry a stable `code`, a `step` and, for scorecard issues, a `side`; the Review **Fix** link uses `step` and no message-text matching is needed.
- No `captain` or `wicket_keeper` flag exists on `result_player`, in the API or in the UI. No `club_result_settings` table, endpoint or dialog exists.
- OpenAPI changes are additive; new services have unit tests and new queries integration tests in the same PR.

## Rollout Notes

- **Order:** migration `044`, the in-place innings reconcile (with its regression test), scorecard save/read and prefill, `ResultValidator` rules (with `code`/`step`), standout rules and the per-entry override columns, link and guest-names; then `ScorecardGrid`, the picker and `StandoutToggle`, Our batting, Our bowling and fielding, Awards, Review and result page. The wizard needs only three registrations in `RESULT_STEPS`.
- **No feature flag.** A result with no scorecard is a valid summary result and behaves exactly as in `100`; the new steps are optional.
- **Super-over spec: `105` (number chosen here: `102` to `104` are taken by `100`'s outline).** Added to `100`'s slice outline and to `docs/roadmap.md` in this change. Open question for `105` (it stays open there, not in 101): **do super-over runs and wickets count toward player statistics?** Convention in international and franchise records is generally to exclude them from career figures, but that is not confirmed for the leagues this platform serves, and it may need to be a per-league or per-club setting. It decides whether entries on a `SUPER_OVER` innings carry a `counts_for_stats` marker (or are filtered by `match_innings.kind` in `103`'s read model), whether they appear on the printed scorecard separately, and whether bowling rows need their own over limit and extras (a super over is six balls per bowler per side, two wickets).
