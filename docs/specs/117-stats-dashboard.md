## Decisions

Settled and binding; nothing here re-opens them.

1. **The existing content is not changed.** The tabs, tiles, cards, tables and form strips of `103`, `115` and `116` keep their DOM, copy and tests. What does change (decisions 11 and 14, and open questions 19 to 21) is **where a manager lands and what sits between the headline figures and the existing team cards**: the Dashboard is added, so the player tabs shift one place right and the team cards move down. This softens the earlier "pure add-on" promise and is called out, not hidden.
2. **No figure is computed in the client** (`frontend.md`). Every plotted value, running average, share, percentage, reference-line value, trend hint and label comes from a server-side series. The client only maps values to pixels, which is drawing, not computing a statistic.
3. **One definition of every number.** The series come from the same pure classes and counting rules as the tabs: `PlayerMatchRowBuilder` and `PlayerStatsCalculator` for a player, `TeamStatsCalculator` for a team. The Dashboard never has its own formula. A definition that does not exist yet (a single match's run rate, a running average, a match's dot percentage, the wicket-type split) is added to the calculator, unit tested there, and the series builder calls it.
4. **Parity.** A Dashboard chart's totals equal the tab figures and the tiles for the same filters (`115`'s sum-of-rows discipline), proven by integration tests.
5. **Published results only**, through `ResultVisibility.PUBLISHED_STATUS`, with the played rule of `103` decision 17 and, for teams, the counted team result of `116` (including `116` decision 15: a forfeit is a loss or a win in the record, with no innings); super-over innings never feed a figure; a derby is one row for a player and one result per team.
6. **Same filters, same scope resolver.** Season, League, Team, Section and Competition on the player page; Season, League and Competition on the team page: the pages' existing filter bars and persisted keys, no new filter UI. Tenancy, 404s, the 403 outside a section-scoped manager's reach and section narrowing are `StatsScopeResolver`'s, **called, not copied**.
7. **Manager-only now; designed for a later player view.** The builders carry no caller or access information; the routes use `canAccessClub`.
8. **The chart library is `@mui/x-charts` v7, approved by the owner on 2026-10-22** (see decision 18 and open question 1). It is a new dependency; the `npm install` and the `CLAUDE.md` approved-addition line were made in the build's dependency commit (rollout step 3, done).
9. **Missing data is shown as missing, never as zero** (`103` decision 7 and 27).
10. **No new menu item, no new brand icon, no migration, no table, no stored statistic.** Compute on read.
11. **Placement (owner, 2026-10-22, superseding the 2026-10-21 "separate lazy Charts tab and collapsed team section").** The feature is named **Dashboard**. On the **player** statistics page it is the **first tab**, in the order **Dashboard, Batting, Bowling, Fielding, Awards & record, Matches** (`?tab=dashboard`). On the **team** page (no tab strip today) the Dashboard is a block **expanded by default, with a Hide / Show control**, placed as decision 14 says. Because the Dashboard is the first tab or first block, **its data loads when the page opens**: there is no lazy-on-open any more. It is **one lightweight server series call per page** (one request, bounded by the caps below, a 200 ms p95 budget, the same counters the page already reads), so opening a page costs one extra small read, not one per chart. The chart code itself may still be split into its own chunk and loaded alongside that request (question 22). Inline charts on the Batting and Bowling tabs are not part of the first release; promoting one or two small sparklines into those cards, once usage shows which charts matter, is a possible later step.

## Problem & Goals

`103`, `115` and `116` show every statistic as a number, a table or a tile. A manager can read a player's average but not see whether it is rising, and can read a team's record but not see that it scores more than it concedes in the second half of the season, or that its bowling is tightening. Trends, shares and comparisons are what a picture answers at a glance, and the data is already computed per match.

This spec adds a **Dashboard** to the player statistics page (first tab: the existing four-tile strip, then the charts, under the existing tab strip, filter bar and basis line) and to the team page (a block under the existing tiles and form strip, hide/show), each fed by one new read-only series endpoint, with a first release of eleven charts and a catalogue of later ones.

Goals:
- **A. Trends and shares at a glance** for one player and one team, on the filters the page already has, **next to** the aggregate figures that stay in place.
- **B. Zero new definitions:** series come from the existing calculators and row builders, so the Dashboard can never disagree with a tab or the tiles.
- **C. Honest by construction:** only what exists is drawn, every chart says how much data it rests on, and an unscored or unrecorded figure is never a zero.
- **D. Accessible, simple and mobile-first:** every chart has an accessible name, a summary sentence and a real table alternative; patterns and direct labels, never colour alone; no dual axes; batting and bowling are labelled and follow the same layout pattern.
- **E. A contained dependency:** one shared card component wraps the library so it can be replaced without touching a page, and the page stays fast (one small request).

## Non-goals

- **Replacing, shrinking, rebuilding, lifting or reordering the existing tab strip, filter bar, basis line, tiles, form strip and cards** (decisions 14 and 16), **changing the content of any existing tab, tile, card or table**, and **inline charts or sparklines on the Batting or Bowling tabs** (decision 11). Their tests pass unchanged; only the tab position (one place right) and, on the team page, the position of the cards below the Dashboard change. (Extracting the Matches tab's four-tile block into a shared part is a refactor with identical output, not a change.)
- **A second form display.** The "Form, last results, newest first" strip is the only form display (decision 17).
- **Dual-axis charts.** None in the first release (decision 15).
- **Export or PDF of charts, image download, sharing a chart.** Deferred, as in `103`, `115` and `116`.
- **A player-facing or public view.** Manager-only now; the endpoints are reusable.
- **Cross-club or cross-league comparisons, benchmarking, league or squad averages.**
- **Predictions, projections, targets, "what do we need" calculations.** (The trend sentences describe the past; they are not forecasts.)
- **A dashboard builder, user-chosen metrics, saved layouts, drag and drop.** "Dashboard" here is a fixed set of charts, not a configurable one.
- **Real-time updates.** Correct on the next read, refreshed by the same query invalidation.
- **Per-ball charts, wagon wheels, pitch maps, over-by-over progression, partnership charts.** Not captured (`101`, `103` decision 3). Dot percentage per match is a bowler-entered total, not per-ball data.
- **A player bowling dots trend** (the player version of the Dot balls card). Team only now.
- **A player wides and no-balls chart.** A player's wides and no-balls stay on the Bowling tab (decision 20); only the team Extras conceded chart exists.
- **Naming the batters a player dismissed, or the bowlers who dismissed a player.** The wicket-type split uses counts of dismissal types only; no other person's name appears (`115` decision 14).
- **Club-level and comparison charts** (for example every team's wins side by side on the Teams tab).
- **Super-over statistics** (`105`), **club time zones** (known gap), **a new table, column, index or migration** unless the plan's `EXPLAIN` proves one is needed.
- **Any change to results capture or to existing calculator figures.** Dots, the extras breakdown and the opposition's bowler-credited dismissals stay optional (`101`).

## User Stories

- As a club manager, I open a player's statistics and, under the same tab strip, filter bar and basis line I already know, I see on the Dashboard tab the familiar Matches / Runs / Wickets / Catches tiles and then six charts in two matching groups, Batting (runs per innings, how you were dismissed, batting average trend) and Bowling (wickets per match, wickets by type, economy rate), so that I can read the totals and judge form at a glance.
- As a club manager, I see chart totals that match the tiles and the Bowling tab for the same filters (runs per innings add up to the Runs tile, wickets per match and wickets by type to the Wickets tile, the economy reference line to the Econ figure), so that I can trust the pictures.
- As a club manager, I can see how a bowler's wickets were taken (bowled, caught, lbw, stumped and so on) and how many of the wickets could not be itemised because a scorecard did not record the dismissals, so that I am never misled by a half-captured match.
- As a bowler or club manager, I can see a bowler's economy rate match by match against their overall economy, and whether it is improving over the last five matches, so that I can tell whether their control is getting better.
- As a club manager, I can see which innings were not out and how many matches have no scorecard, so that I am never misled by a half-captured match.
- As a club manager, I open a team's statistics and see its headline tiles and form strip, then a Dashboard of results split, runs scored against conceded, run rate trend, extras conceded and dot balls, and I can hide it, so that I keep the page as short as I like.
- As a club manager, I can see for the last 30 matches how many extras the team conceded and of which kind, and whether the share of dot balls is rising, with a note of how many matches the figures rest on, so that I can tell whether bowling discipline is improving without being misled by matches where it was not entered.
- As a club manager, I change League, Season, Section, Team or Competition once, and the tiles, the Dashboard and the tabs all follow the same filters, so that a chart and the figure beside it always agree.
- As a club manager, I can switch any chart to a table of the same values, so that I can read exact figures or use the page without seeing the picture.
- As a section-scoped manager, I see the Dashboard only for players and teams inside my sections, as on the tabs.
- As a club manager, I know that unpublishing a result changes every chart on the next read.

## Data Model Changes

**None: no table, column, migration or index.** Series are computed on read from the same counters and facts as `103`, `115` and `116` (`match_innings` extras columns from `044`/`101`; `bowling_entry.balls`, `runs`, `wickets`, `dots` and `wickets_source` and `batting_entry.bowler_result_player_id` from `044`/`045`, all existing).

### What is plotted: the series

All series are chronological (oldest first) unless a donut, carry the match date, the opponent name resolved as `115`/`116` do (the other team's name in a derby, no person named anywhere), and the match and result ids so a table row can link to the result page.

**Player series (`PlayerDashboardDto`)**, from the per-match facts and `PlayerMatchRowBuilder` rows (`115`), one counted match each, over the same counted matches as the tiles (decision 16). First release, in the display order of decision 20:

| Series | Content | Parity target (same filters) |

Feeds both **Wickets per match** and **Economy rate** | Σ `wickets` = **Wickets tile**; Σ balls, runs = Bowling tab |

|---|---|---|
| `results[]` | per counted team result: `matchId`, `resultId`, `date`, `opponentName`, `derby`, `forfeit` (conceded or received), `result` (`WON`, `LOST`, `TIED`, `DRAWN`, `NO_RESULT`), `ownRuns`, `ownWickets`, `ownBalls`, `oppRuns`, `oppWickets`, `oppBalls`, `ownRunRate`, `oppRunRate` (null when no balls). ### Wickets by type (player chart 5, decision 19)

**What data exists (`101`, `103`).** The Wickets tile is `Σ bowling_entry.wickets` over the player's counted bowling rows: the stored effective value, `wickets_source` `DERIVED` or `MANUAL`, bowler-credited only (`103` figure 6). The type of each wicket is not on the bowling row. It lives on the **opposition's batting card of the same innings**: each `batting_entry` carries `how_out` and `bowler_result_player_id` (the player credited with the dismissal, a player of the fielding side of that innings; `101`, migration `045`). `DismissalType.isBowlerCredited()` is true for BOWLED, CAUGHT, CAUGHT_AND_BOWLED, LBW, STUMPED and HIT_WICKET, and false for RUN_OUT, HANDLED_BALL, OBSTRUCTING_FIELD, HIT_BALL_TWICE, TIMED_OUT, RETIRED_OUT (the non-bowler wickets), NOT_OUT, RETIRED_NOT_OUT and DID_NOT_BAT. The bowler link is optional: the warning `BOWLER_WICKETS_DIFFER_FROM_DISMISSALS` is "checked only when every bowler-credited dismissal of that innings names a bowler", so a card may leave the bowler blank. The Bowling tab of `103` already reports `dismissalModes { type: count, notRecorded }`; the donut and that figure are the same definition (the plan makes the calculator the single place, and a parity test asserts they agree).

**The exact rule (in `PlayerStatsCalculator`, unit tested; the builder only calls it).** For each of the player's counted **bowling innings** (one `bowling_entry` row on a regular innings of a counted result, the same rows the Wickets tile sums):
1. `w` = the row's stored `wickets` (exactly the Wickets-tile contribution).
2. `c[type]` = the number of `batting_entry` rows on the **opposite** (batting) side of that same innings with `bowler_result_player_id` = this player's `result_player` and a **bowler-credited** `how_out`. Run-outs, retirements, obstructions, handled ball, hit ball twice and timed out are never credited to a bowler, so they are never counted here, and a `NOT_OUT`/`DID_NOT_BAT` row is not a dismissal.
3. `c` = Σ `c[type]`. **If `c <= w`**: the innings contributes `c[type]` to each type and `w - c` to **Not itemised**. **If `c > w`** (a `MANUAL` bowling row whose wickets are lower than the dismissals credited to the player): the stored `wickets` is authoritative, so the innings is **not itemised at all** and contributes all `w` to Not itemised. An innings with no opposition batting card contributes all `w` to Not itemised.
4. Totals: `types[type] = Σ` over innings; `notItemised = Σ`; `total = Σ w`.

By construction **Σ types + notItemised = total = the Wickets tile**, whether or not the cards are present; no wicket is invented or dropped, and the split never uses a different wickets definition from `103`. A derby (the player on both sides) sums the rows of both sides, as `103` does. `CAUGHT_AND_BOWLED` is its own slice (question 25(g)).

**DTO (`wicketTypes`):** `types[]` of `{type, label, count, percent}` (percent of `total`, 1 decimal, server-computed, null when `total` is 0) in a fixed order (Bowled, Caught, Caught and bowled, LBW, Stumped, Hit wicket), only types with `count > 0` drawn but all returned for the table; `notItemised {count, percent}`; `total`; `itemised` (= Σ types); `coverage {wicketsItemised, wicketsTotal, inningsItemised, inningsWithWickets}`. The caption reads **"N of M wickets itemised"** (hidden when N = M). The remainder slice is **"Not itemised"**, the same label and grey tone as the team Extras card. The donut has total wickets in the centre, a legend with counts and percentages, patterns per slice, subtitle **Bowling**.

**View as table:** columns **Dismissal type, Wickets, Share**; a row per type with count > 0, then Not itemised, then a Total row equal to the Wickets tile. **States:** no wickets taken → "No wickets taken in these matches." (distinct from a missing bowling card: "No bowling scorecard was captured for these matches."); every wicket not itemised → a single grey Not itemised slice with "0 of M wickets itemised" and a one-line hint that the opposition's batting cards do not name the bowler; partly itemised → the donut with the grey slice and the "N of M" caption.

### Economy rate (player chart 6, decision 20)

**Definition (the `103` Econ, unchanged).** Economy Rate = Runs Conceded / Σ (balls / `ballsPerOver` of that match), runs per over where an over is **that match's** number of balls, **2 decimals, `HALF_UP` once**, intermediate overs at 10 or more decimal places (`103` "Precise definitions"). Runs Conceded is `Σ bowling_entry.runs` (wides and no-balls charged to the bowler are in it; byes, leg byes and penalty runs are not, `101`). `bowling_entry.balls` and `runs` are not-null columns on a bowling row, so a row's figures are always recorded; a figure is **undefined (null, a dash and a chart gap, never 0) when the balls are 0**. A match in which the player has a bowling row with 0 balls bowled and any figures is not charted for economy.

**Per point (`bowlingMatches[]`, shared with Wickets per match):** one counted match in which the player has a bowling row, oldest to newest, last 30. Per match: `balls` and `runs` summed over the player's bowling rows in it (a derby sums both sides, as `103`), `ballsPerOver` of that match, `wickets`, `maidens`, and `economy` = runs / (balls / `ballsPerOver`), 2 decimals, null when `balls` is 0. The tooltip shows overs (through `oversFormat`), runs and wickets; the table shows the same. A `DID_NOT_BOWL` or `NO_CARD` match is not a point.

**`economySummary`:**
- `overallEconomy`: the player's Economy Rate over the whole filtered scope, **computed by the same calculator method as the Bowling tab's Econ figure** (Σ runs / Σ overs over all counted bowling rows, not the mean of the match figures), 2 decimals; null with no balls. It is the dashed reference line, labelled with its value, and **equals the Econ figure on the Bowling tab for the same filters**.
- `matchesCounted` (matches in the window in which the player bowled), `matchesWithEconomy` (those with defined economy), `totalPoints`, `shown`.
- **Trend hint:** when at least three matches with defined economy exist in each half compared, `recentEconomy` and `earlierEconomy` (the pooled economy, Σ runs / Σ overs, of the last five bowled matches with defined economy against the five before them) and a `direction` (`UP`, `DOWN`, `FLAT`, flat within 0.25 runs per over), all computed through the calculator. Otherwise the hint fields are null and no sentence is shown. The text reads, for example, "Last 5 matches: 5.1 runs per over, down from 6.0 in the 5 before." with the direction also in words (up, down, level), and, because lower is better, no colour judgement beyond the "Lower is better" label.
- The caption reads **"Economy shown for N of M matches"** (N = `matchesWithEconomy`, M = `matchesCounted`), hidden when N = M.

**Card:** a single-axis line with markers (`primary.main`), oldest left, a broken line across a match without economy (a gap, not a zero), the dashed reference line at `overallEconomy`, **"Lower is better"**, subtitle **Bowling**, half width beside Wickets by type. It mirrors Batting trend above it. **View as table:** Date, Opponent, Overs, Runs, Wickets, Econ; a dash plus "Not recorded" wording for a null Econ (never 0); rows link to the result page. **States:** no bowling scorecard → the shared no-bowling-card message; bowled but no match with defined economy → "Economy cannot be shown: no balls bowled in these matches."; fewer than three matches per half → no trend sentence. **Parity:** `overallEconomy` equals the Bowling tab's Econ figure; Σ `runs` and Σ balls over the points (untruncated) equal the Bowling tab's Runs Conceded and balls; each point's `economy` is the calculator's per-match value (hand-checked in the unit test). The player's **wides and no-balls are not in this chart** (decision 20); the Bowling tab keeps them.

### Extras conceded (team chart 10, decision 15a)

One point per **counted team result with a regular opposition innings** (our bowling innings: `batting_side <> side`, `kind = 'REGULAR'`; a forfeit, a no-ball-bowled no-result and super-over innings never appear), oldest to newest, last 30. The innings columns are `101`'s `wides`, `no_balls`, `byes`, `leg_byes`, `penalty_runs` (each `int null`, **in runs**; a wide that runs four is 5 wides) and the stored total `extras`. The rules (`101`, `116`):

- When **any component is non-null** the server derives `extras` as the sum of the non-null components; with **no component** (a summary-level result) `extras` is whatever was entered, a total only.
- A component is **recorded** when it is non-null; a null component is not recorded (never 0), exactly as `116`'s "each with its own coverage" breakdown.

Per point (`extrasConceded[]`):

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| `wides`, `noBalls`, `byes`, `legByes`, `penaltyRuns` | the innings' stored component, or **null when not recorded** |
| `itemised` | true when at least one component is non-null |
| `notItemised` | the stored `extras` total when **no** component is recorded but `extras` is non-null (the grey **"Not itemised"** segment), otherwise null |
| `total` | the innings' stored `extras` (equal to the sum of the recorded components when itemised); **null when the innings recorded no extras at all**, which is a **gap** (no bar), never 0 |

`extrasSummary` carries `matchesCounted` (our bowling innings in the window), `matchesWithExtras` (total non-null), `matchesItemised`, `totalPoints`, `shown`, and per-type recorded counts. The caption reads "Extras recorded for N of M matches" (and "itemised for K" when K differs from N). The drawn bar per match is the stacked components (legend: Wides, No-balls, Byes, Leg byes, Penalty runs, Not itemised) and a tooltip shows the type values and the total. **View as table columns:** Date, Opponent, Wides, No-balls, Byes, Leg-byes, Penalty, Total; "Not recorded" for a null component or a null total, and "Not itemised" noted beside the Total of a total-only match. Label on the card: **"Lower is better"**. Parity: summed over an untruncated scope, each type's total equals the Extras card's figure for that type with the same coverage, and Σ `total` equals Extras conceded.

### Dot balls (team chart 11, decision 15b)

One point per counted team result with a regular opposition innings, same window and ordering. Definitions (added to `TeamStatsCalculator`, unit tested there):

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| `ballsBowled` | the sum of `balls` over the innings' bowler rows (`bowling_entry`) of our players, the denominator; null when the innings has no bowler rows |
| `dots` | the sum of `bowling_entry.dots` over those rows, **only if every bowler row that bowled at least one ball recorded `dots`**; otherwise null for that match (a gap, not a partial sum, which would understate dots). A zero recorded by the manager is a real 0 |
| `dotPercent` | `dots / ballsBowled x 100`, 1 decimal, `HALF_UP` once; null when `dots` is null or `ballsBowled` is 0 |

`dotBallsSummary` carries `matchesCounted`, `matchesWithDots`, `totalPoints`, `shown`; `averageDotPercent` (the **pooled** figure over the charted matches: Σ dots / Σ balls bowled of those matches, 1 decimal, drawn as the **dashed reference line**; null with no charted match); and the **trend hint**: when at least three charted matches exist in each half compared, `recentAverage` and `earlierAverage` (the pooled dot percentage of the last five charted matches against the five before them) and a `direction` (`UP`, `DOWN`, `FLAT`, flat within 0.5 percentage points), all computed in the builder through the calculator. Otherwise the hint fields are null and no sentence is shown. The bars are one per charted match, simple, on a single 0 to 100 % scale; a match without dots is a gap, not a zero bar. Label: **"Higher is better"**. **View as table columns:** Date, Opponent, Balls bowled, Dot balls, Dot %; "Not recorded" for nulls.

**Caps and ordering.** Bar and line charts of per-match values return the **most recent 30 points** (`window`), with `totalPoints` and `shown` ("last 30 of 52 innings"). Running trends and donuts cover **every** counted match up to a server cap of **300** (newest kept; `truncated: true` and a "since <date>" caption beyond it; parity is stated for untruncated scopes). Named constants. The response is small (a few kilobytes of numbers), which is what makes loading it with the page acceptable (decision 11). Note for decision 16: the sum-equals-tile guarantee holds for the untruncated scope; where a per-match chart shows only the last 30, its caption says so and the table alternative and the donuts carry the full totals; the **economy reference line and trend are always over the full filtered scope**, so the line equals the Bowling tab's Econ even when only 30 matches are drawn; the integration tests cover both the windowed and the full path.

## API Contract

Base `/api/v1/manage/clubs/{clubId}`; `@PreAuthorize("@access.canAccessClub(authentication, #clubId)")`; `PlayerStatsController` and `TeamStatsController` gain one method each; services `PlayerDashboardService` / `PlayerDashboardServiceImpl` and `TeamDashboardService` / `TeamDashboardServiceImpl` (`@Transactional(readOnly = true)`); DTOs only.

| Endpoint | Access | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /players/{playerId}/stats/dashboard?seasonId=&leagueId=&teamId=&sectionId=&competition=` | club manager (`canAccessClub`), section-narrowed in the service | All player Dashboard series for the filtered scope, in one response |
| `GET /teams/{teamId}/stats/dashboard?seasonId=&leagueId=&competition=` | club manager (`canAccessClub`); a team outside a section-scoped manager's reach is 403 | All team Dashboard series for the filtered scope, in one response |

Filters, 404s, 400 (bad `competition`) and 403 are exactly those of `GET /players/{playerId}/stats` and `GET /teams/{teamId}/stats`, with the **same parameter values the page's `FilterBar` holds** (decision 16). A subject with nothing counted is a 200 with empty series and zero `basis`. The existing `filter-options` endpoints drive the filters. Each DTO carries `scope`, `basis`, `coverage` per series, `window`, `truncated` and `notCaptured`. `openapi.yaml` is regenerated by hand, additions only.

**Reading the extras, bowler and dismissal rows.** Team extras components come from the opposition `match_innings` row the team query already joins. Team dots and balls come from `bowling_entry` rows of the counted results, summed per result in SQL as additive counters (`sum(balls)`, `sum(dots)`, `count(*) filter (where balls > 0)`, `count(dots) filter (where balls > 0)`). The player facts query reads, per bowling row, the stored `balls`, `runs`, `wickets` and `maidens` (the `115` `BowlingFact` already carries them) and the match's `balls_per_over`, and, for Wickets by type, **per bowling innings** the counts from the opposite side's `batting_entry` rows, `count(*) filter (where bowler_result_player_id = :bowlerRp and how_out = '<type>')` for each bowler-credited type (additive, stored columns only). All by statistics facts queries (the second permitted reader, under the shared `PUBLISHED` and `PLAYED_RESULT` constants); the `c <= w` rule, the economy and overs maths and every split are applied by the calculator from those counters. No new index is expected (`batting_entry.bowler_result_player_id` is already indexed, `045`).

**When the UI calls it (decision 11).** The player page requests the Dashboard when the Dashboard tab is the active tab (on open, or when selected if the page landed elsewhere); the team page requests it when the page opens with the block shown, and not while it is hidden. It is the same one request in both cases, sent in parallel with the page's own stats read using the same filter state, so the Dashboard does not delay the tiles and a filter change refetches both.

**Why one request per page, not the paged match list.** The charts share one scope and one set of counters; one request cannot show two charts from different moments. Assembling series in the browser from `GET .../stats/matches` pages is **rejected**: it moves definitions into the client (decision 2), downloads fields the charts do not need, and the running average would depend on how many pages were loaded.

### Where the code lives and how it reuses

1. **`PlayerDashboardSeriesBuilder`** and **`TeamDashboardSeriesBuilder`** (pure, `service.support`, no Spring, repository, entity, caller or access information) are the only places that shape a series. The player builder takes the same inputs as `PlayerMatchRowBuilder` (or its rows) and calls `PlayerStatsCalculator`; the team builder takes the per-result counters `116` reads and calls `TeamStatsCalculator`. **A single-match rate, running value or split the calculator lacks (including the dot percentage, the pooled averages, the trend averages, the wicket-type split and the per-match economy) is added to the calculator and unit tested there, never written in a builder; the economy maths reuses the calculator method that produces the Bowling tab's Econ.**
2. **Facts.** The services call the existing header and fact reads of `115` and `116` (an unpaged variant bounded by the 300 cap, newest first, then reversed), so no counting rule is duplicated; any new SQL only selects stored columns under the shared `PUBLISHED` and `PLAYED_RESULT` constants (`StatsCountingSql`) and joins the existing repository ArchUnit rule. The plan confirms whether the `115` header query can be called with `size = 300` or needs a sibling method.
3. **Statement count** fixed per request, pinned by a query-count test.
4. **Performance (it loads with the page).** The plan runs `EXPLAIN` on a seeded club (all-time, no filters), records plans and timings in the PR, and proposes 200 ms p95 and a payload budget (for example 60 KB uncompressed at the cap); the plan reports the real size. If a measured problem appears, the fallback is to fetch after first paint, not to drop parity.
5. **Names.** No response names a person other than the subject; opponent names are club or free-text names; bowler and batting rows are counted per innings and no batter or bowler is named.
